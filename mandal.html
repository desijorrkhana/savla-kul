Shri Savla Bhavik Mandal

The body that looks after Siriyal Maa's devasthan, keeps the kul's records, and gathers the Savla households for Pehdi and Juvar each year.

What the Mandal does

  • Maintains the devasthan of Shri Siriyal Mataji at Bhinsara and conducts its daily seva-puja and festivals.
  • Conducts the Pehdi every year at Mumbai and the Juvar every year at Bhinsara, on the traditional mahurat.
  • Appoints and supports the Bhatt Raja, and keeps the Chopda — the genealogical register of the kul from 1565 to the present — so any Savla can trace their lineage on request.
  • Records births, marriages and deaths of Savla households in the Chopda through the Bhatt Raja.
  • Safeguards the Mata's ornaments, vessels and the community's old records and photographs.
  • Carries out and publishes research on the history of the kul — these pages are part of that.

How the Mandal is organised

The Mandal has grown from an informal gathering of families to a community of about 500 households in Kutch, Mumbai and abroad. It is now being constituted as a registered public religious trust under the Gujarat Public Trusts Act, with the deity as owner of the devasthan and its ornaments, a board of trustees drawn from Kutch and the diaspora, and a general body of one representative per household. Trustees serve fixed terms and are recommended by the general body.

How to contribute

1
Records
Old chopdas, family trees, letters, photographs of the Mata or the old temple, Pehdi and Juvar photographs from any year. Originals stay with you; the Mandal takes a scan.
2
Research
Reading the pattavali and Digdarshan, locating Sonapur, visiting Jalore or Kanthkot, obtaining the Savla chopdi from the Jamnagar Bhatt Raja. Join a historical trip.
3
Seva and funds
Towards the devasthan, the Pehdi and Juvar, the Bhatt Raja's lifetime pension, and the digitisation of the Chopda. Details on request from the trustees.
