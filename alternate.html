Alternate accounts

Versions of our origin that are on record but do not fit the 1255 line. Kept here so nothing is lost, and so anyone can weigh them.

Why this page exists

One main story, with the rest kept honestly

The Utpatti page tells the account the Mandal considers most credible: Devaḍaji Chavda's conversion at Patan in Samvat 1255 and the Savla nukh descending from his son Jetsinh. The sources also carry other versions. Some differ in date or place; one tells a different conversion story altogether. Rather than force them into one narrative, they are set out here as alternates. If future research shows one of them to be the stronger account, the main page will be revised.

Alternate 1 · The place

Jaisalmer, not Patan

All four sources give Samvat 1255 for Devaḍ Chavda's conversion. They differ on where it happened. The Barot notebook and the Chheda vanshvruksh say Anhilpur Patan, on Bhadarva Sud 5. The Anchalgachha Mahoti Pattavali and the Anchalgachha Digdarshan (¶346) say Jaisalmer. Jayasimhasuri was active in both regions, so neither is impossible; the Digdarshan's own survey of his life (¶382) also notes that dates in these records drift by a year because Gujarati, Marwadi and Kutchi samvats begin in different months.

The Mandal follows the family record for the place, since the Barot tradition is the kul's own memory and the poster carries it independently. The Jaisalmer reading is kept here. One detail worth watching: the Digdarshan (¶381) records another of Jayasimhasuri's conversions, Rao Keshav of Kotda in Marwad, on Bhadarva Sud 5 of Samvat 1259 — the same tithi our record gives for Devaḍ.

A second Dedhia origin in the Digdarshan

The Digdarshan (¶349–350) also preserves a variant for the sister nukh: a Dedji who founded a village named Dedhiya near Osian; the Parmars of Osian took it in Samvat 1100; and in Samvat 1254 Jayasimhasuri converted the Dedhiya-kul families, who then entered the Oswal jnati. The book sets this beside the Devaḍ account without choosing between them.

Alternate 2 · The Sonapur story

Samat Sahu and Acharya Ajitsimhasuri

Samat Sahu, also called Sapar Sahu, a descendant of Vanraj, held the village of Sonapur. With his fighting men he waylaid travellers. One night a Shatrunjay sangh led by Acharya Ajit Muni Suri camped by the Sonapur lake, and Samat and his men looted it. The Acharya spoke the stambhan mantra and Samat and his men stood frozen by the water. At dawn his mother came and wept at the Acharya's feet. He said he would release her son if he gave up violence and took the dharma of ahimsa. She gave her word; Samat was freed; he and his household accepted the Jain dharma, and in his home Sarayal Devi was established. "Savla Sahu na Savla thaya."

This story is in our Barot notebook, pages 1–3. The Anchalgachha pattavali (pp. 215–217) tells the same event from the sangh's side, naming the lord Samarsimha, a Chavda kshatriya, and the Acharya as Ajitsimhasuri, sixth pattadhar, whose time as gachhanayak was about VS 1316–1339. The pattavali names no gotra for Samarsimha's descendants.

Why it is listed as an alternate

Ajitsimhasuri's period is sixty to ninety years after Devaḍaji's conversion in 1255. If the Savla nukh already existed from Jetsinh, a founding conversion two or three generations later does not fit logically. Possible readings: a Chavda branch that had stayed Rajput was brought into the fold later and merged into the Savla memory; the Barot tradition fused two Acharyas and two events; or the Sonapur account belongs to the Kuldevi's establishment rather than to the kul's origin. The Mandal has not settled this. The story is kept here, in full, until it is.

Alternate 3 · The vansh lists

Savla under Rathod?

The Barot notebook's tables of the 84 nukhs by Rajput origin place Savla under both Rathod and Chavda, and Dedhia under both Parmar and Chavda. Barot vansh-to-nukh lists are known to be inconsistent. The Chavda attribution rests on the lineage narrative, not on these tables; the Rathod entry is noted here and not followed.
